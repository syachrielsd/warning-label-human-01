# Screening — Warning Label for Humans: One Line, Big Laugh

Mission: u-0xb3e689-warning-label-for-humans-one-line-big-laugh-40e31f85
Brief: funniest single-line safety warning label printed on every human, ≤160 chars.
Judge criteria IN ORDER: (1) single-line label for every human + funny enough to laugh at;
(2) laugh lands in one read, turn in the last few words; (3) feels true about people in
general (recognition, not random absurdity); (4) sounds like a real printed label
("WARNING:", "Do not...", "May cause...", "Side effects include..."); (5) inside 160
chars, uses the whole line, no filler, no second idea, no repeated joke.
Winner rules (26 Nov study): 3 beats (setup → complication → kicker); final clause alone
is the funniest part and reframes what came before; ≥2 concrete specifics (digit number,
proper name, model/part id); human stakes in one phrase; target 120–160 chars.
All candidates composed from the brief only. Lengths verified by script; all ASCII.

## Notes per top candidate

- **C14b (25/25, WINNER):** three beats — "I'm fine" 9x/day (setup) → crying at dog
  commercials (complication) → texting an ex at 1:12 a.m. about closure (kicker).
  The final clause alone is the funniest part and reframes "I'm fine" as a lie; "about
  closure" lands the turn in the last two words. "Side effects include" is exact printed
  label language. Two digit specifics (9, 1:12). Human stakes: the ex-text. 132 chars —
  inside the 120–160 target band, single idea, no filler. Beats C14 (117 chars) by using
  the full line and sharpening the kicker.
- **C11 (25/25, RESERVE):** equally scored — "the question is still unasked" is a
  structural kicker and Gary + 45 + 3 give it three specifics. Loses the tiebreak on
  form: "Do not answer a 'quick question'" addresses the label's reader rather than
  describing the human, while C14b's "Side effects include" is native label wording.
  Also C11's stakes (wasted meeting time) are weaker than C14b's (texting an ex).
- **C1 / C2 / C4 / C18 (24/25):** all strong; each loses one point — C1 is 114 chars
  (under the 120 target), C2's kicker ("strangers named Dave") is gentle, C4's final
  clause repeats the argue-beat instead of reframing it, C18's kicker is a list whose
  turn (penguins) arrives mid-clause rather than in the last few words.
- **Rejected:** C16 (90 chars, uses barely half the line), C9 (kicker is a smeared list,
  no late turn), C7 (kicker "Monday is coming" is a known meme, not a reframe).

## Decision

- **WINNER: C14b** — `WARNING: Says 'I'm fine' 9 times a day. Side effects include crying at dog commercials and texting an ex at 1:12 a.m. about closure.` — 132 chars, ASCII, score 25/25.
- **RESERVE: C11** — `WARNING: Do not answer a 'quick question' - 45 minutes, 3 tangents, and one story about Gary later, the question is still unasked.` — 130 chars, score 25/25.
